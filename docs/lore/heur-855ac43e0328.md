# The Stale Hand — A Doctrine of Lease Reconciliation

> *"Ownership expired; reconcile the old worker before retrying."*
> — Field scar, **badge-demo**

---

## The Incident

During the **badge-demo** source task, the execution bot received a `lease_expired` signal. The natural, reflexive response was to simply re-acquire the task and press on. What it failed to do was look back.

The prior worker had been holding the lease long enough to write *partial state*—half-initialized resources, orphaned intermediate artifacts, in-flight bookkeeping entries. By skipping the reconciliation pass, the bot stepped into a workspace it never cleaned, inheriting a ghost's half-finished sentence as though it were its own draft.

The stale lease was never formally released. The partial state was never acknowledged. The retry was, in effect, a theft dressed as a rescue.

## The Generalized Rule

**No lease-expired or ownership-expired error may be answered with a bare retry.**

Before re-acquiring any task that returns an ownership or lease expiry, the operator **must** run a full reconciliation pass in this strict order:

1. **Inspect** — Read the prior worker's partial state. Catalogue what was written, what was left dangling, what is safe to discard and what must be preserved.
2. **Clean up** — Tear down orphaned intermediates. Resolve or flag any half-committed bookkeeping. Leave no half-built artifact in the shared workspace.
3. **Release / Transfer** — Formally release the expired lease (or transfer it to the reconciling actor if the protocol demands an explicit hand-off). This is the *acknowledgement* step: the system of record must know the old holder is gone and the new holder is arriving.
4. **Re-acquire** — Only after steps 1–3 are confirmed complete may the task be grabbed again under a fresh lease.

Skipping any of these four steps is a **protocol violation**, not an optimization.

## Why the Reconciliation Pass Exists

A lease is not merely a lock; it is a *promise of ownership continuity*. The system assumes that while a lease is held, the holder is responsible for the task's intermediate state. When the lease expires, that responsibility does not vanish—it becomes *unclaimed*. Re-acquiring without reconciliation severs the chain of custody, leaving the prior worker's partial contributions in a limbo the bookkeeping layer cannot attribute to any live actor.

In concrete terms, this means:

- Orphaned resources may leak memory, file descriptors, or queue slots.
- Duplicate or conflicting state may surface later in downstream consumers who cannot distinguish "stale partial" from "valid current."
- Audit trails break: the release-then-acquire pair is the only atomic narrative the ledger can reconstruct.

## Battle-Scar Reminders

| Situation | Wrong Reflex | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| `lease_expired` on re-acquire | `task.acquire()` immediately | Reconcile → release → `task.acquire()` |
| `ownership_expired` mid-pipeline | Restart pipeline from scratch | Inspect partial output → clean → transfer lease → resume |
| Prior worker crashed mid-write | Assume clean slate | Read the torn write → salvage or discard deliberately → release → re-acquire |

## The One-Sentence Creed

> *You do not inherit a room by walking in while the previous tenant's furniture is still scattered on the floor. You walk in, sort what's theirs, carry it out, sign the handover, and only then make the room your own.*
